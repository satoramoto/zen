Butterick’s Practical Typography

Matthew Butterick

Get the everyday reading conditions right, then refine emphasis and punctuation.

Reference: the author’s online book ↗
16 / B1 · Body text

Start with comfortable body text

Compare one of the core variables: line spacing.

Good notes are an invitation to return. Leave enough room for a thought to unfold, and enough structure to find it again. A clear paragraph asks little of its reader. It gives each line space, keeps a steady rhythm, and lets the words carry the meaning. When the page feels calm, attention can settle on what matters.

Only line spacing changes: 105% → 135%. Georgia at 17 px and the maximum 64 ch measure stay fixed.

About the principles and references

These 18 “axioms” are paraphrased teaching takeaways from our discussion, not verbatim statements or exhaustive summaries of the books. All examples are original interface studies.

References link to public publisher descriptions, the official contents, the Albers Foundation’s teaching account, and Butterick’s full online chapters. The publisher overviews establish topic alignment; they do not verify every adaptation against a page in the full book.

Two independent review agents checked every example against its stated principle and public reference. Both passed all 18 after revisions. Browser checks were performed separately by the coordinator.
